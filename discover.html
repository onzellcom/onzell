Read, relax, imagine

Two-minute stories

The little lantern

At the edge of a garden, a lantern felt too small to light the whole path. One evening, a child placed it beside the first stepping stone. Soon another lantern appeared, then another. The path was bright not because one lantern did everything, but because each lit the next step.

A little reminder: Small acts can make a big difference.

The surprise picnic

Two friends planned a perfect picnic. The wind folded their blanket, clouds covered the sun, and their sandwiches looked a little squashed. They laughed, moved beneath a tree, and invented a game of naming shapes in the clouds. Later they agreed: the day had been perfect after all—just not as planned.

A little reminder: Sometimes joy arrives in a different package.

The baker's extra cookie

Each afternoon, a baker saved one cookie for anyone who needed a kind moment. Some days no one came. Then a shy neighbor knocked and asked if she could leave a thank-you note instead. By winter, the little jar beside the cookies was full of notes, and the whole street felt a little warmer.

A little reminder: Kindness has a way of growing.

Easy and delicious

Recipes for everyday life

Simple ideas with approachable ingredients. Check ingredient labels for allergies and follow safe food-handling practices.

Breakfast · About 10 minutes · 2 servings

Yogurt and berry breakfast cups

Ingredients: 2 cups plain yogurt, 1 cup washed berries, ½ cup granola, 1 sliced banana, optional drizzle of honey for people older than 1 year.

  1. Wash berries and slice the banana.
  2. Divide yogurt between two bowls or cups.
  3. Add fruit and top with granola immediately before serving.
  4. Adjust textures for young children and check dairy, nut and granola allergens.
Lunch or dinner · About 25 minutes · 4 servings

Lemony chickpea and vegetable bowls

Ingredients: 2 cans chickpeas (15 oz each), drained and rinsed; 1 cucumber; 2 tomatoes; 1 bell pepper; 2 tablespoons olive oil; 1 lemon; parsley; cooked brown rice or quinoa; salt and pepper to taste.

  1. Prepare rice or quinoa following package instructions.
  2. Wash and finely dice vegetables; drain and rinse chickpeas.
  3. Toss vegetables and chickpeas with olive oil, lemon juice and chopped parsley.
  4. Serve over warm grains; refrigerate leftovers promptly.

For very young children, serve appropriately sized and softened components rather than whole firm legumes or large raw pieces.

Food safety guidance: FoodSafety.gov's four steps.

Everyday well-being

Healthy habits at every age

Kids & families

Make room for active play, regular sleep routines, water breaks and relaxed family meals. Keep activities age-appropriate and focus on enjoyment, not strict targets.

Adults

Break up long sitting periods, find movement you enjoy, choose balanced meals when possible and make time to reconnect with people you care about.

Older adults

Enjoy safe, comfortable movement and social activities. Balance and strength activities may help independence, but the right approach depends on health and mobility needs.

General educational content only—not diagnosis, treatment or individualized medical advice. Consult a qualified clinician about personal health concerns or changing an exercise routine with a health condition. CDC activity guidelines for different ages.
