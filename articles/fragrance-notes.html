Find the fragrance notes that feel like you

A welcoming guide to the scent families behind perfumes—and how to discover your preferences.

Understand the scent story

Perfume is often described in top, heart and base notes. Top notes make the first impression, heart notes become noticeable as the fragrance develops, and base notes tend to linger. These stages overlap, and how a perfume wears can vary by skin, climate and concentration.

Citrus notes often feel bright; florals can range from airy to rich; woods may feel grounded; gourmands can suggest vanilla, spice or sweets.

Test slowly

Try one or two scents at a time and allow them to settle before deciding. A perfume that feels sharp at first might become softer after thirty minutes. Compare how it smells on a testing strip with how it wears on your skin.

Rather than choosing only by brand or popularity, think about when and where you want to wear it—workdays, warm weather, evenings or special occasions.

Build a thoughtful collection

You do not need a crowded fragrance shelf. A versatile daytime scent and one you enjoy for evenings may be enough. Store bottles away from direct sun and heat to help preserve their character.
